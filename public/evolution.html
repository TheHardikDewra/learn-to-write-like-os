The arc

Fifteen years, measured twice

You said to weight the latest work most, and that is right - but only if you know what actually changed. Two corpora were measured separately: twelve books over twelve years, and 528 blog posts over fifteen. They agree on some things and disagree on one big one. The disagreement is the most useful thing on this page.

Unified

What changed in both books and blog

These four moved the same direction, the same decade, in both media. That makes them habits of the hand rather than choices about audience - which means they transfer to anything you write.

Semicolons. Down 84% on the blog and 83% in the books. He confirmed it himself in a footnote: he now uses one only in the rarest case.

Divergent - read this twice

The pronoun inversion is blog-only

On the blog, over fifteen years, "you" nearly halved while "I" doubled and "we" almost quadrupled. He stopped instructing the reader and started standing beside them. The mechanism is refutability: a "you" sentence can be rejected in real time. A "we" sentence cannot be rejected without self-indictment. An "I" sentence is testimony and cannot be argued with at all.

His books did the opposite. "we" peaked in 2019 at 9.98 per 1k and fell to 1.91 by 2024. In books the pronouns are set by genre, not by year: his 2014 memoir runs 43.9 "I" per thousand words, his 2024 memoir 44.4. A decade changed nothing.

The trap

Do not copy the inclusive "we" into a one-shot piece for strangers. It is priced against a fifteen-year relationship with a returning audience. Without one it reads as presumption. Use it in serial writing - a newsletter, a blog you actually keep - and use "I" and "you" everywhere else.

Live finding

He switched off the em dash entirely

Not a drift. A switch. His last ten consecutive posts, November 2025 to July 2026, contain zero em dashes. The twelve posts before them contain 58, including one post with thirteen. Anyone reading the fifteen-year trend line would conclude em dashes were rising, and would be adopting a habit he has since abolished.
