Vocabulary you can borrow in a week. Sentence shapes take a month. These are the structural habits, and they are what actually separates his 2024 prose from his 2014 prose. Each one states the finding, then what to do about it on Monday.

Tested claim

The Stephen King adverb rule, checked against 950,000 words

"The road to hell is paved with adverbs." It is the most repeated piece of writing advice there is, so it is worth knowing whether the writer you admire actually follows it. He follows half of it, and the half he ignores is the half most people obsess over.

What he cuts

Manner

Adverbs saying HOW something was done: angrily, gently, quietly, hurriedly. Down 31% between his early and late books, to about one every 750 words. On dialogue tags he is near-absolute - five in a whole 2024 book.

What he keeps

Measure

Adverbs saying HOW MUCH or HOW EXACTLY: completely, exactly, nearly, barely, merely, simply. These he uses freely. "Simply" is his single most-used adverb in every era of both corpora.

The rule to actually follow

Do not hunt for -ly words. Hunt for manner adverbs. If the word tells the reader how, cut it and fix the verb instead. If it tells them how much, keep it. And never put one beside "said" - across his late corpus he writes plain "said" 165 times against 36 uses of every other tag combined.
