The verdict for your work

Take the blog register for almost everything: the pronoun arc, the one-image spine, the 25% turn, the disclaimer before the ask, conjunction openings, and the hard stop. Take from the books only their sentence-length discipline. And note the target keeps moving: his 2026 posts are tighter than both his older blog and his own books. Aim at the last column.

Measured across 45 recent posts

The pronoun arc

This is the most copyable structure he has. Split any post of his into ten equal slices and the pronouns move in a fixed shape: he opens in "I", drains it, ramps "you", and saves the imperatives for the very end. He earns the right to instruct rather than opening with it.

Opening

Testify

"I" runs at 44.6 per 1k in the first tenth, its highest point. You pay for the piece with your own story before you ask anything.

Middle

Diagnose in "we"

The failure is always "we", never "you". A "you" fault can be rejected on sight. A "we" fault cannot be refused without self-indictment.

Close

Hand over the verb

Imperatives run 0.9% in the first tenth and 5.3% in the last, a near sixfold ramp. The commands live at the end or nowhere.

The load-bearing fact

The argument is never delivered continuously. It arrives in the gaps between narrative blocks, and he rarely runs more than about 450 words of pure abstraction before dropping back into a scene.

For your actual job

Mapped onto a landing page

You design and write landing pages. The same skeleton maps almost one to one, and the pronoun arc is the part most pages get backwards - they open by accusing the visitor and close by talking about themselves.
